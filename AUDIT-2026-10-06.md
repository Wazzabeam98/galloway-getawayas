# Audit — 6 October 2026

Read-only audit of everything merged since 4 October (PRs #289–#338), the
database security on every table/view/bucket touched in that range, the four
v1-2026-10-03 agreements and the privacy policy against the code, and a walk of
the main guest and host pages at phone and desktop width.

Done from `origin/master` (SHA `2c97990c`, fetched fresh) on branch
`audit/2026-10-06`. No code changed, no PR opened, nothing written to
production. Test data created on TEST to walk the host side was a single seed on
its own `@gallowayeditor.test` domain and was removed again (`--reset`); no
bookings were created. Every finding below was proved, not guessed — each says
how.

**Headline:** the money path is in good shape. The four high-scrutiny money PRs
(discounts #334, stay rules #335, back-to-back checkouts #336, pets/safety #337)
are careful and I verified the discount/deposit/cancellation maths live in the
browser — they are correct to the penny. The database lockdown (#298) and the
account-erasure migration (#319) hold up: no way found for anyone to read or
write data that isn't theirs. The real problems are two: one correctness gap in
**how account deletion decides it's safe to run** (it can strand a paying guest),
and two places where the **legal agreements now disagree with the code** — one of
them about money.

## Findings, worst first

### 1. Account deletion can erase a live experience provider and strand a paying guest  — CONFIRMED, HIGH

**What I saw.** "Delete my account" and "Deactivate my account" use *different*
safety checks, and delete's is the weaker one.

- Deactivation refuses while any live work remains. `deactivate_own_account`
  calls `account_deactivation_blockers(uid)`, which reads **bookings,
  service_orders AND service_enquiries** — "an experience with an authorised
  (requested, card held) or confirmed" upcoming order blocks it
  (`supabase/migrations/20261002091040_deactivate_and_reactivate_account_reversibly.sql:61-107`).
- Deletion (erasure) refuses only on **bookings**. `admin_anonymise_account`'s
  guard counts `bookings where (guest_id = uid or host_id = uid) and status in
  ('pending','confirmed') and check_out >= current_date` and nothing else
  (`supabase/migrations/20261005094931_complete_account_erasure_across_all_personal_data.sql:80-90`).
- The two paths are independent. The account page offers "I want to delete my
  account" directly — it is **not** gated behind deactivating first — and
  `deleteAccount()` just POSTs to `/api/account/delete` with no blocker check
  (`app/account/page.tsx:420-428`). The route calls `anonymise_own_account`
  straight through (`app/api/account/delete/route.ts:80`); its own comment admits
  the scope: *"It refuses if there are still live bookings to cancel."* Bookings
  only.

**How I proved it.** Read both guards in the migration SQL and both UI paths.
The asymmetry is unambiguous in the code: the exact list of statuses and tables
each one checks is quoted above. `service_orders` and `service_enquiries` appear
in the deactivation guard and are absent from the erasure guard.

**Who it hurts, how badly.** An experience provider or tradesperson who has **no
upcoming stay** but **does** have a confirmed/authorised experience order or a
live enquiry can type DELETE and go through. The scrub then anonymises their
`service_providers` row — `business_name` → "Removed provider", contact email/
phone nulled, `venue_lat/venue_lng` nulled, photos cleared
(same migration, lines 124-144) — while **keeping the paid `service_order`** that
now points at a wiped provider. The guest who paid for that chef / sauna / guide
is left with a confirmed booking no one can fulfil and no contact for the person
due to turn up. The mirror case also exists: a **guest** with an upcoming
experience order but no stay can erase, nulling their own `guest_name`, `phone`
and **`allergy`** on an order the provider still has to deliver — wiping a food
allergy off a live food order is a safety problem, not just a data one. The
deactivation flow was clearly built to prevent exactly this; erasure walks round
it. Money + stranded third party = high.

**One-line fix.** Make `admin_anonymise_account` (or `/api/account/delete`
before it) run `account_deactivation_blockers(uid)` and refuse while any live
reservation, order or enquiry remains — the same wall deactivation already uses.

### 2. Guest Terms say the damage deposit is part of the price; the code and the site say it isn't  — CONFIRMED, HIGH (money/legal)

**What I saw.** Guest Terms clause 3.4
(`components/legal/agreements/text/guest.ts:41`):
*"The price shown includes the nightly rate and any fees the Host has set
(cleaning, pets, extra guests, damage deposit)."* Combined with the agent framing
in clause 4.1 ("We collect payment from you as agent for the Host"), this reads
as: the platform collects the damage deposit as part of the price.

**How I proved it.** Read the clause directly. Then checked the code: the damage
deposit is **never** in the total — `lib/pricing.ts` sums nights + extra-guest +
pet + cleaning only, `damage_deposit` is not referenced; in checkout it only
flips `keepCard` (`app/api/stripe/checkout/route.ts:371`), nothing is charged.
And the guest UI says the exact opposite in plain words — the booking widget:
*"Collected by your host at the property and returned after your stay. It isn't
part of the total above and we don't take it."*
(`components/BookingWidget.tsx:898-899`), echoed in
`components/ThingsToKnow.tsx:174` ("Held and collected by the host"). The
listing page I walked shows the agent line and no deposit in the £136 total.

**Who it hurts, how badly.** Both sides. A guest who reads the terms may believe
the deposit is already paid and refuse the host's request to collect it; and the
clause implies the platform holds deposit money, which muddies the agent-only
liability position Liam is trying to keep (the agent-vs-principal question in the
"not done" list). The binding document contradicts the product's own screens.

**One-line fix.** Drop "damage deposit" from the 3.4 list and add a sentence that
the damage deposit is collected and held by the Host directly, outside the price
paid through the platform.

### 3. Tradesperson Agreement starts the free trial at the wrong moment  — CONFIRMED, MEDIUM

**What I saw.** Tradesperson Agreement clause 3.2
(`components/legal/agreements/text/tradesperson.ts:37`): *"New Tradespeople get a
free trial of six months from the date your listing goes live."*

**How I proved it.** The code starts the six-month clock at the **first enquiry
sent**, not at go-live: `trialEndsAt()` / `shouldStartTrial()` / `planTerms()` in
`lib/serviceProviders.ts` ("stamped when the FIRST ENQUIRY IS SENT to him, not
when he is approved"), and the recruitment FAQ agrees ("once we send you your
first enquiry", `lib/recruitFaq.ts:209,242`). So the agreement is out of step
with both the code and the marketing.

**Who it hurts, how badly.** The reality is *more generous* than the document
(the clock doesn't start until a lead actually arrives), so a tradesperson could
quote clause 3.2 and argue billing began — or should begin — earlier than the
system charges. It understates the benefit and invites a dispute. Low financial
risk, but it's a binding doc contradicting the billing code.

**One-line fix.** Change 3.2 to "six months from the date we send you your first
enquiry," matching `trialEndsAt` and the FAQ.

**What I saw / proved.** The "What is NOT done" list (item 5) and the project's

a bug that was already fixed. **Fix:** delete item 5 from the "not done" list.

## Database security — tables, views, buckets, functions in range (no holes found)

Checked every migration from `20261004*` to `20261005*` (the #289–#338 range),
their grant/RLS guard tests, and the function lockdown. **Can an authenticated
or anon user read or write data that isn't theirs via anything in this range?
No** — and the lockdown is enforced by guard tests, not just documented.

- **#298 function lockdown** (`20261004133346`): revokes EXECUTE on every public
  function from anon/authenticated by default, re-grants exactly seven, and fixes
  the root cause (postgres default privileges drop the browser roles).
  `order_reviewable_by` was rewritten to gate on `p_guest = auth.uid()`, closing a
  caller-supplied-id probe. `tests/function-grants-guard.test.ts` fails the build
  if any public function becomes browser-executable off the allow-list. Nothing
  essential broke.
- **New columns** (sleeping/neighbourhood, `show_precise_location`, `max_pets`,
  `checkout_tasks`, `checkout_note`, `guest_safety`, `venue_lat/lng`,
  `admin_hidden_at`, `messages.automated`, `listing_reports.target_*`): all either
  SELECT-only to the browser and written via the service route, or fully walled by
  RLS, and each is bucketed in the column-grant guard tests that compare the live
  grant to the intended decision in both directions.
- **`listing_private` view** (recreated three times in range): every recreation
  re-applies the revoke-all-then-grant-select block, so the "Supabase grants ALL
  on a new view → browser can write the base table" class stays closed.
- **bookings INSERT policy** (`20261004101500`): strictly tighter — now also
  requires the listing be `published`, closing the "book a taken-down listing"
  hole. **`submit_service_provider` / `reactivate_account`**: SECURITY DEFINER but
  gated on `auth.uid()` ownership or granted to service_role only — no IDOR.

Two notes, neither a vulnerability:
- **Cosmetic:** `my_account_deactivation_blockers()` is granted to authenticated
  in `20261004130329` but revoked by #298's blanket revoke (which runs later and
  wins). Nothing calls it from the browser, so nothing breaks — but the two
  migrations state opposite intents. Drop the grant line from `20261004130329` to
  make them agree.
- **Enforcement location:** the DB-half of the grant/column guard tests only runs
  when `SUPABASE_TEST_DB_URL` is set (pre-push / local), not in CI. The real live-
  grant gate therefore depends on the push happening from a wired-up machine.

## The ten things to fix first

1. **Close the account-deletion guard gap (Finding 1).** Make erasure refuse
   while any live order or enquiry remains, the way deactivation already does.
   This is the only finding that can hurt a paying stranger — fix it first.
2. **Fix Guest Terms 3.4 (Finding 2):** remove "damage deposit" from the included-
   price list and say the host holds it directly. Money + liability, binding doc.
3. **Fix Tradesperson 3.2 (Finding 3):** trial starts at first enquiry, not go-live.
4. **Have a solicitor confirm the agent-vs-principal / VAT position** now that all
   four agreements and the code consistently assert "agent, not principal" — this
   is the unresolved legal question, and the damage-deposit wording (Finding 2)
   feeds straight into it.
5. **Bump the privacy policy "Last updated" date (Finding 5)** to when erasure
   actually shipped (5 Oct 2026).
6. **Delete the stale "10% refund fee" line from the "not done" list (Finding 4)** —
   it's already fixed in the terms and the note is misleading.
7. **Still the biggest untested thing: run the payout engine end to end.** Nothing
   in this range touched it; it remains the largest money path never executed.
8. **Resolve the `my_account_deactivation_blockers` grant contradiction** between
   `20261004130329` and #298 (cosmetic, but make the two migrations agree).
9. **Wire the grant/column guard DB-half into CI** (or confirm every push to
   master goes through a machine with `SUPABASE_TEST_DB_URL`), so the live-grant
   gate can't be skipped.
10. **Move the three `bg-stone-50` pages to `bg-slate-50`** (home, area, arrival)
    as a deliberate, standalone job — long-standing platform-consistency debt.
