# One-page summary

*Read this page and you will understand the shape of the business before reading
anything else.*

**What the business is.** Galloway Getaways is a website through which people book
self-catering holiday cottages in Dumfries & Galloway, Scotland. Alongside the
cottages, it now also lets guests book local **experiences** during their stay (a
private chef, a sauna, a cake, a photographer), and it **introduces** hosts and
guests to local **tradesmen** (plumbers, electricians, cleaners and so on). The
company takes a commission on some of these, a monthly fee from some tradesmen,
and nothing at all on others. It is a small business preparing for a soft launch
of around ten properties; the people involved know each other personally.

**There are four distinct money flows, and they are not the same in law:**

1. **Cottage bookings.** A guest books a cottage and **pays Galloway Getaways**.
   The company **holds the whole amount**, then pays the property owner (the
   "host") the day after the guest arrives, keeping **10% commission**. To the
   bank and on the card statement, **Galloway Getaways is the one taking the
   payment**. *Who contracts with whom:* the stay itself is described as a
   contract **between the guest and the host**; Galloway provides the booking and
   payment service. (This combination — we hold the money and are the payment
   merchant, but say the stay contract is guest-to-host — is one of the things the
   solicitor needs to weigh.)

2. **Guest experiences.** A guest books, say, a chef for one evening of their
   stay. The guest **pays the chef**, through Galloway's checkout, and **the chef
   — not Galloway — is the one taking the payment** (their name is on the
   receipt). Galloway takes **10% as a fee** and **never holds the rest** — the
   money goes to the provider. The guest is **told on screen, before paying**,
   that Galloway is taking payment on the provider's behalf and is not the
   provider. *Who contracts with whom:* the experience is a contract **between the
   guest and the provider**.

3. **Tradesman jobs.** A host or guest is **introduced** to a tradesman for a job.
   **No money for the job passes through Galloway at all** — the tradesman quotes
   and is paid **directly** by the customer. *Who contracts with whom:* the job is
   a contract **between the customer and the tradesman**; Galloway only makes the
   introduction.

4. **The tradesman subscription.** Most tradesmen pay Galloway **£20 a month** to
   be listed and receive introductions (after a free period). Galloway keeps the
   whole £20; there is no commission on their jobs. *Who contracts with whom:* a
   straightforward contract **between the tradesman and Galloway**.

**The single most important distinction for tax and liability** is between flow 1
and flow 2. On **cottage bookings** Galloway is the merchant of record and holds
the full booking value. On **guest experiences** the provider is the merchant of
record and Galloway only ever holds its own 10% fee. They sit on opposite sides of
the line, and it is determined entirely by how each payment is set up.

Everything after this page is the detail behind it.

## Question 1 — VAT

**What we want to achieve:** to account for VAT on the **10% commission we take**,
not on the **gross value of every booking** — and, separately, to know what counts
as our **turnover** for deciding whether we must register at all.

**Why it is not obvious:** the four flows are structurally different, and on the
biggest one (cottage bookings) we look, on paper, like a principal handling the
whole booking value.

**The facts, per flow** (from Part B):

1. **Cottage bookings.** The guest pays **us**. **We are the merchant of record**
   and the receipt and card statement show **Galloway Getaways**. We **hold the
   full booking amount** in our own account, then pay the host the day after
   check-in, keeping 10%. The guest is not told, at the point of paying, that we
   are only an agent for the host — they simply pay "us" for "the stay". **This is
   the same position Airbnb is in** — Airbnb is merchant of record and holds the
   money — and Airbnb accounts for VAT on its **service fee**, not on the whole
   booking. So the question is: **can we do the same, and if not, what is different
   about us?** (Candidate differences for you to weigh: Airbnb's terms expressly
   cast it as agent and its guest-facing fee is separated out, whereas ours are
   not; and Airbnb operates at a scale and with contractual scaffolding we do not
   yet have.)

2. **Guest experiences.** The guest pays **the provider**; **the provider is the
   merchant of record** and the receipt/statement show **the provider**. **We never
   hold the gross — only our 10% fee reaches us.** The guest **is** told, before
   paying, that we take payment on the provider's behalf and are not the provider.
   This flow already looks like agency on its face.

3. **Tradesman jobs.** **No money reaches us.** The tradesman is paid directly.
   There is nothing here to account for except, indirectly, the subscription.

4. **Subscription.** The tradesman pays **us** £20/month; **we are merchant of
   record** and keep all of it. This is a straightforward supply by us to the
   tradesman.

**What we are asking you, explicitly and as two separate questions:**

- **(a) What do we account for VAT on in each flow** — our commission/fee, or the
  gross the customer paid? In particular, on cottage bookings, does being merchant
  of record and holding the gross force VAT onto the whole booking value, or can we
  be treated as agent and account on the 10% as Airbnb does? If we cannot, what
  specifically would we need to change (in the terms, in what the guest is told, in
  how the payment is structured) to be able to?

- **(b) What counts as our turnover for the VAT-registration threshold?** Is it the
  total value of bookings that flow through our account (cottage gross +
  subscriptions), or only our commissions and fees (cottage 10% + experience 10% +
  subscriptions)? We are **not currently registered**, and whether we cross the
  threshold depends entirely on this answer — the two readings differ by roughly a
  factor of ten on the cottage side.

**Stated plainly for the record:** **no VAT registration number appears anywhere in
the platform, and no VAT is calculated in any flow** — there is no tax rate and no
VAT line in any charge or any price. If a registration exists it is only in the
company's own records. **This needs confirming from the company records before
anything is relied on.**

## Question 2 — Liability for the service itself

**The scenario:** a chef booked as a guest experience gives someone food poisoning;
or a tradesman introduced through the site botches a job and causes damage or
injury. **Who is liable — Galloway Getaways, or the provider/tradesman?**

**What we actually do** (please treat every one of these as a fact that may move the
answer):

1. **We take the payment.** On guest experiences we run the checkout (though the
   provider is merchant of record and the money settles to them). On cottage
   bookings we are merchant of record and hold the full amount. On tradesman jobs
   we handle no money.
2. **We take a commission** — 10% on experiences, 10% on cottage stays; £20/month
   from subscription tradesmen; nothing per job from tradesmen.
3. **We approve who gets listed.** Providers and tradesmen cannot appear or take
   payment until we have manually approved them.
4. **We collect declarations, but do not verify certificates at sign-up.** On the
   live site today, a provider/tradesman declares their **trade registrations**
   (e.g. Gas Safe, OFTEC, food-business registration) by entering the numbers; we do
   **not** check the certificates at sign-up — verification, if it happens, is a
   later manual act by us. A **public liability insurance declaration** (a
   self-certified tick-box, also unverified) has been built for guest-experience
   providers but is **not yet live on production** — it exists on a development
   branch and has been applied to our test environment only. **So today we collect
   no public liability insurance declaration on the live site.**
5. **We set no prices for experiences or jobs.** The provider or tradesman sets
   their own price; our cut is a commission on it, never a markup.
6. **What the customer is told differs by flow.** On **guest experiences** the
   guest is told, before paying, *"Galloway Getaways takes the payment on their
   behalf and is not the provider."* On **cottage bookings there is no equivalent
   line** — the guest simply pays us for the stay.

**What we are asking you, explicitly:**

- How does each of the six facts above affect the answer? In particular, do
  **taking the payment**, **taking a commission**, and **approving who is listed**
  push us from "introducer" towards "principal" — and does the guest-experience
  "we are not the provider" disclosure meaningfully help, while its **absence on
  cottage bookings** hurts?
- Is there anything we currently do that makes us a **principal** (liable for the
  service) rather than an **introducer/agent** (liable only for running the
  platform honestly)?
- What would we need to change — **in the terms, in the payment/booking flow, or in
  our insurance** — to sit where we want to sit (an introducer/agent, not the
  provider of the service)?
- **What insurance should we ourselves be carrying** as a platform in this position
  (e.g. professional indemnity, public liability, and any cover tied to our
  handling of guests' money on the cottage flow)? Assume we currently hold none
  specific to these risks unless the company records say otherwise.

**The honest gap:** the difference between introducer and principal is the whole
question, it turns on facts we have listed rather than on a position we have picked,
and we have not picked one. Please tell us where these facts actually put us, and
what to change to move.
